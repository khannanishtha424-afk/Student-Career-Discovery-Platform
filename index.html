01 Executive Overview • PRD Summary
PRD v1.0 • Founder Deliverable
Product Vision

“Build the mirror first, the map second, and earn trust at every step.”

Consolidating research across 35 students, teachers, and parents into an evidence-based guidance platform.

The Problem Statement

Students in grades 9 to 12 are asked to choose streams, subjects and careers before they understand their own strengths, interests and preferences. The people around them mean well, but their advice can be uninformed, biased, or not right for the student.

Schools do not yet give enough structured guidance. Career decisions end up shaped by outside influence, pressure and guesswork instead of evidence about the student.

Target Users

Primary User

Students in Grades 9–12

Target window: Grades 9–11 prior to stream lock-in.

Secondary Users

Teachers and Parents

Class guidance toolkits & trusted progress roadmaps.

Product Goals (7 Strategic Pillars)

1

Give every student a clear picture of their strengths, interests and aptitude through an assessment that is the entry point to the product.

2

Turn assessment results into one living profile per student, shareable with teachers and parents with the student's consent.

3

Offer career recommendations that truly fit each student, explained in plain language and based on evidence rather than pressure or guesswork.

4

Show every student clear steps from their current grade through roadmaps, college information and skill gap analysis.

5

Help teachers guide students with class profiles, discussion guides, a toolkit and a path to counsellors.

6

Give parents a light summary with the roadmap and salary and market context that they can trust.

7

Earn trust at every step by showing how recommendations are made and keeping counsellor review in the loop.

Guiding Principles (6 Core Tenets)

Build the mirror first, the map second.

Self knowledge comes before planning.

Make trust a feature, not a footnote.

Explain how each recommendation was made.

Speak to the student directly.

Students drive their own decisions.

Help teachers, do not replace them.

Position as a tool that supports teachers.

Lead with fit, show salary as context.

Skill and interest match first.

Start early.

Target grades 9 to 11, before stream selection.

04
Synthesis

Key Insights (7 Cards)

1. The core problem is self-knowledge, not information.

Stat: 0% have taken an assessment

Every student surveyed has never taken an assessment, 41% do not know their strengths, and 59% rate their self-knowledge at a flat 3 out of 5. Students are choosing careers without a mirror.

2. Students drive their own decisions, so the product must speak to them directly.

Stat: 59% self-influenced

59% say they influence their own choice most, and only 6% name teachers. Parents and teachers support the decision but do not control it.

3. Parents are more confident than their children.

Stat: 4.0 vs 3.2

Parents average 4.0 on confidence, students average 3.2. With only 5 parents this is a signal, not a proof, but it points to a gap that a shared profile or report could close.

4. Trust, not interest, is the parent barrier.

Stat: 80% cite trust

80% of parents would try an assessment platform and 80% name trust and credibility as the thing that could stop them. A recommendation they do not believe is worth nothing.

5. Teachers want structure more than AI.

Stat: 100% want profiles

All 13 teachers want structured student profiles, but only 31% rate an AI platform at 4 or 5. The product should be positioned as a tool that helps teachers, not one that replaces them.

6. Skills and passion beat salary.

Stat: Skills 2.35, Salary 2.76

Skills and passion tie for first in the student ranking, with salary third. Recommendations should lead with fit and show pay as context.

7. Start early.

Stat: 65% in grades 9–10

65% of students are in grades 9 and 10, and teachers ask for guidance to begin at grade 9. The best window is before stream selection, not after.

Integrity

Limitations & Risks (3-Column Matrix)

Issue Why it matters How to handle it
⚠ Small samples, especially 5 parents Parent findings are directional only Run a second parent round before locking parent features
⚠ No interview data in the forms Qualitative layer rests on open text answers Add interview notes if they exist, or run 5–8 short interviews per group
⚠ Parent answers look inconsistent 80% say counsellors are how they help, yet 60% say they never sought counselling Clarify question wording in next round
⚠ Teacher answers spread across many options 46–62% fall in "mix of everything" category, so single winners are weak Use theme-level conclusions, not exact percentages
⚠ School, city and board not captured Results may not generalise Add school type and location fields to the next version of each form
